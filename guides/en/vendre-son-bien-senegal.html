Selling Land or a House in Senegal: Documents, Price, Timelines

Published on 2026-08-09

A sale that drags costs the seller money, not just time. A property listed for too long starts to worry buyers, who assume a hidden problem and negotiate accordingly. Most slow sales are slow for one plain reason: the file was not ready when the property went on the market.

Gather the documents before you list

Put everything together before the first viewing, not when a buyer commits. A serious buyer left waiting three weeks for a document starts to doubt, or finds something else.

  • The ownership document: freehold title, lease or deliberation, in its original form. The land title status governs everything that follows.
  • Your identity document, and the exact name as it appears on the title. A spelling difference between the two can be resolved, but it takes time.
  • The plan and survey if you are selling land.
  • Tax documents required of sellers. Your notary will say which ones apply to your situation.
  • The agreement of co-owners where the property comes from an estate or is jointly held. This is by far the leading cause of a blocked sale.

Setting a price that holds

An inflated price does not correct itself. It keeps buyers away for months, then forces a cut below true value to make up lost ground. A price set too low draws attention too, and raises the suspicion of a defect.

Work from comparable sales: same municipality, same property type, similar size, recent transactions. Online listings give a first order of magnitude, provided you compare like with like. Condition, exact location and market timing account for the rest. Write to us for a valuation of your property: we know the prices being paid in the areas we cover.

What actually sells a property

Sharp photographs taken in daylight, with rooms empty or tidy. A listing that states the size, the number of rooms, the land title status and the precise neighbourhood. A contact who answers the same day.

Showing the land title status in the listing saves everyone time. Buyers for whom that status does not work never make the trip, and those who do come already know what to expect.

The notary's role for a seller

Notaries do not work for the buyer alone. They confirm that you are legally able to sell, draft the deed, calculate what is owed and secure the payment. A sale settled directly between individuals, with no notarized deed, exposes the seller as much as the buyer: it can be challenged years later.

Realistic timelines

Between agreeing a price and the final signature, count in weeks rather than days. The notary's checks, obtaining administrative papers and, often, the buyer's financing each take their own time. A buyer who promises to sign within a week is rarely describing reality.

A complete file from the outset remains the only lever that genuinely shortens those timelines.

Frequently asked questions

Can I sell land held under a deliberation?
Yes, though the procedure and the guarantees offered to the buyer differ from those of freehold title, and the price reflects that. State the status clearly from the start.

Will I owe tax on the sale?
Capital gains tax may apply depending on your situation and how long you have held the property. Ask your notary when you list, not at signature.

Can I sell from abroad?
Yes, through a notarized power of attorney, by the same mechanism as a purchase. Our guide on buying from abroad sets out the procedure; it transposes to a sale.

This article gives general guidance and is neither legal nor tax advice. The rules that apply depend on your situation: consult a notary before committing.
